Conventions

Sign conventions are choices, not facts, and different books choose differently. These are the ones in force here.

  • $\cos(kx - \omega t)$ travels towards $+x$; $\cos(kx + \omega t)$ travels towards $-x$. Follow a point of constant phase to check.
  • Oscillations use $e^{+i\omega t}$ and the real part is taken at the end. Quantum mechanics conventionally uses $e^{-i\omega t}$ instead; nothing physical changes, but signs in intermediate steps flip.
  • The transform is $F(k) = \int f(x)e^{-ikx}\dd x$ with the $1/2\pi$ on the inverse. Some books split it as $1/\sqrt{2\pi}$ on each; the physics is the same and the width relation is unaffected.
  • Displacements are measured from equilibrium. In section 2.2 that is what lets the equilibrium spring forces be ignored.
  • $R$ and $\mathcal{T}$ are amplitude ratios. The energy fractions are $R^{2}$ and $(Z_2/Z_1)\mathcal{T}^{2}$, which is why $\mathcal{T}$ may exceed one without anything being wrong.
  • Eigenvectors have no fixed length. Any multiple of an eigenvector is an eigenvector, so $(1,1)$ and $(3,3)$ describe the same mode.
Check yourself
  1. In $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ and in $v = \omega/k$, what is $k$ in each?
    Answer
    A spring constant in newtons per metre in the first, a wavenumber in radians per metre in the second. Check the units: $\sqrt{(\mathrm{N/m})/\mathrm{kg}}$ is $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, while $(\mathrm{rad/s})/(\mathrm{rad/m})$ is $\mathrm{m/s}$. The units always settle it.
  2. A problem says "a string of length $L$ with tension $T$". What should you check?
    Answer
    Whether $T$ is a tension in newtons or a period in seconds. This site writes tension as $\Ten$ to avoid the clash, but most books do not. The units, or the formula it appears in, decide: $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$ only makes sense with a force.
  3. Where on the site does $\lambda$ not mean a wavelength?
    Answer
    Section 1.3, where it is an eigenvalue. Section 2.2 uses the eigenvalue machinery on a physical system and sidesteps the collision by writing $\omega^{2}$ throughout instead of $\lambda$.

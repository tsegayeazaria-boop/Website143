Waves and oscillations, built from the ground up

Every idea Problem Set 0 assumes, and all of the mathematics underneath it, derived here in full. Nothing is asserted and left standing: if an equation appears on this site, you can watch it being built one term at a time.

Who this is for

It assumes AP Physics C — Newton's laws, energy, Hooke's law, the basics of electricity and magnetism — and single-variable calculus: derivatives, integrals, integration by parts, and Taylor series. It assumes you have seen $i=\sqrt{-1}$ and that $e^{x}$ is its own derivative.

It assumes no linear algebra at all. Section 1.3 starts from what a vector is and ends at eigenvalues, orthogonal function bases and the reason quantum mechanics is written the way it is. It is the longest section on the site and the one everything else leans on.

Where a new idea is needed — a partial derivative, a matrix, a complex exponential — it is defined on the page that first needs it, in terms of things you already know.

How to read a page

Every subsection follows the same four beats, in the same order:

  1. What is going on — the physics in plain words, before any symbols.
  2. The mathematics — the equation, derived rather than quoted.
  3. A worked example — real numbers with units, carried to an answer.
  4. Something to move — a demonstration you drive with sliders, with one line telling you what to look for.

Each subsection closes with a single bold sentence: the one thing to keep if you keep nothing else. Each page closes with three to five questions whose answers are hidden until you ask for them.

The derivations move

Derivations are not printed as a wall of algebra. Each one is pinned to the screen while you scroll, and every term slides from where it was to where it goes — the exponent of $x^{2}$ comes down and lands in front, a term crosses the equals sign and its sign flips, an exponential cancels from both sides and fades away. Scroll forward to run a step; scroll back to run it in reverse. Every derivation also has an All steps at once panel if you would rather read it as a list.

If you have asked your system to reduce motion, the derivations are shown as that list instead, with nothing moving.

Offline

Everything works from a local copy except the mathematics typesetting, which is loaded from a public CDN. Without a network the equations appear as LaTeX source and the rest of the page — including every demonstration — still works.

Suggested reading order

Straight down the list is the safe route: nothing appears before the thing it needs. The times are for a first careful read, demonstrations included.

Two shortcuts, if you are in a hurry: 2.1 can be read straight after 1.2 without 1.3, and 2.7 and 2.8 need only 1.1. Nothing else can be safely reordered.

Four symbols that mean two things

Physics reuses letters, and pretending otherwise causes more confusion than it avoids. Four are worth knowing before you start.

  • $k$ is a spring constant in sections 2.1 and 2.2, and a wavenumber from 1.4 onwards. Every page says which at the top.
  • $T$ is a period everywhere. String tension is written $\Ten$ so the two can never collide, and $\mathcal{T}$ is the transmission coefficient in 2.4.
  • $\lambda$ is a wavelength everywhere except 1.3, where it is an eigenvalue. Section 2.2 sidesteps the clash by writing $\omega^{2}$.
  • $I$ is an intensity in 2.7 and 2.8 only.

The full list, with units, is in the quick reference.
